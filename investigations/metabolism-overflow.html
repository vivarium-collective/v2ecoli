🧬 Biology — the mechanism this investigation models When a fast-growing E. coli takes up glucose faster than its respiratory chain can fully oxidize the carbon, it spills the excess as acetate — aerobic "overflow" metabolism —…

When a fast-growing E. coli takes up glucose faster than its respiratory chain can fully oxidize the carbon, it spills the excess as acetate — aerobic "overflow" metabolism — even with oxygen present. The fraction of consumed carbon leaving as acetate is ~0 below a critical growth rate and rises roughly linearly above it: a clean, repeatedly-measured, strain-robust relationship (Vemuri 2006 in chemostat; Basan 2015 by titrating glucose-transporter expression). Basan's proteome-allocation account makes it a story about a respiratory ceiling: past some carbon influx, respiring is more protein-expensive per ATP than fermenting, so the cell spills.

That makes overflow an unusually good test of whether a whole-cell model encodes a real physiological constraint rather than just a stoichiometry. A growth-max FBA with no proteome or respiratory-capacity limit has no reason to spill carbon — it will respire everything it takes up. So the question this investigation asks is diagnostic, not merely comparative: drive the carbon knob, watch what comes out, and find out WHERE the overflow physiology is or isn't encoded.

The answer for the baseline FBA arm is that it is not encoded at all: acetate is identically zero across the whole titration. That is the expected answer, and it localizes the gap — which is what the instrument is for.

Biology

The v2ecoli baseline FBA exhibits NO aerobic acetate overflow anywhere in a glucose-uptake titration: acetate is identically 0.0 mmol/gDCW/h at all six caps, so the acetate-carbon yield stays flat at 0 while the Vemuri 2006 reference rises to ~0.25 above an onset of 0.408 /h. This confirms the pre-registered prediction (and the ketchup-baseline-comparison prior that v2ecoli secretes 0.0 acetate per 100 glucose where the KETCHUP cores secrete ~+71): overflow is a constraint phenomenon, and unconstrained growth-max FBA respires fully rather than spilling carbon.

THIS FINDING REFUTES THIS STUDY'S OWN SECOND DESIGN ASSUMPTION — that a glucose-uptake titration moves the model along a growth-rate axis comparable to the references'. It is FALSE for v2ecoli. This is a refutation of our premise, not a disagreement with a reference.

Across a 5.6x uptake range the emergent growth rate moves only 0.954 -> 0.925 /h, non-monotonically. The cause is by design, not a bug: division runs with d_period=True (the vEcoli default), so it fires on the D-period replication clock and the dry-mass threshold is deliberately ignored (the `threshold=nan fg` in every DIVISION log line; see v2ecoli/steps/division.py:181-190). Mean division time — and hence the mu this study reads out — is therefore set by replication timing, not carbon supply, and cells divide on schedule at ~695 fg regardless of how tightly glucose is capped.

Consequence for the instrument: an acetate-vs-growth-rate card cannot be driven by a carbon knob in this model. All six sweep points land in a ~0.03 /h band, entirely above the reference onset, so the `overflow-absent-across-titration` verdict is graded on a degenerate x-range. Adding seeds or generations will not help — the axis is structurally pinned, not noisy.

Under a binding glucose cap the model creates carbon. At cap=1 it takes up 6.0 mmolC/gDCW/h as glucose and secretes 11.83 mmolC/gDCW/h of carbon — 11.62 of it as CO2 — while still growing at 0.954 /h and therefore also building biomass carbon. This trips the assumption-free `violation_C_created` flag, which compares secreted C-exchange against C uptake and needs no biomass C-content assumption.

The effect is inverted with respect to carbon supply: CO2 output RISES as glucose is restricted (11.62 / 5.00 / 1.82 mmol/gDCW/h at caps 1 / 2 / 6), and the carbon closure fraction degrades monotonically as the cap tightens (1.11 -> 7.93 across caps 6 -> 1). At the slack cap the books nearly close; the tighter the constraint, the more carbon appears from nowhere. Undiagnosed here. Candidate mechanisms: FBA infeasibility handling under a binding bound, non-FBA replenishment of metabolite pools, or per-gDCW flux normalisation against a stale mass.

Emergent biomass yield exceeds the stoichiometric ceiling at every cap, and the excess grows as carbon is restricted: Yxs = 0.92 / 1.04 / 1.29 / 1.71 / 2.59 / 5.30 gDW per g glucose at caps 6 -> 1, against a measured literature band of 0.355-0.444 (Varma 1994, Kavvas 2022, LaCroix 2015, Long 2017 — the four `kind: measured` rows of the ecoli-sources basal__biomass_yield slot) and a stoichiometric ceiling of 0.538 (Varma 1993, the slot's single `kind: theoretical_max` row). Even at the slack cap, where the FBA runs at its unconstrained optimum and the carbon books nearly close, the yield is ~1.7x the ceiling; at cap=1 it is ~10x.

This is the same defect the basal vs_literature card already reports (biomass yield over the limit), but the titration shows it is not a fixed offset — it is a function of how hard the carbon constraint binds, which points at the same bookkeeping problem as `carbon-created-under-binding-uptake-cap` rather than at a mis-parameterised biomass composition.

Conclusion synthesis

Read-only synthesis derived from the study's canonical fields (findings, limitations, follow-up proposals).

Claims
  • The v2ecoli baseline FBA exhibits NO aerobic acetate overflow anywhere in a glucose-uptake titration: acetate is identically 0.0 mmol/gDCW/h at all six caps, so the acetate-carbon yield stays flat at 0 while the Vemuri 2006 reference rises to ~0.25 above an onset of 0.408 /h. This confirms the pre-registered prediction (and the ketchup-baseline-comparison prior that v2ecoli secretes 0.0 acetate per 100 glucose where the KETCHUP cores secrete ~+71): overflow is a constraint phenomenon, and unconstrained growth-max FBA respires fully rather than spilling carbon.
  • THIS FINDING REFUTES THIS STUDY'S OWN SECOND DESIGN ASSUMPTION — that a glucose-uptake titration moves the model along a growth-rate axis comparable to the references'. It is FALSE for v2ecoli. This is a refutation of our premise, not a disagreement with a reference.

    Across a 5.6x uptake range the emergent growth rate moves only 0.954 -> 0.925 /h, non-monotonically. The cause is by design, not a bug: division runs with d_period=True (the vEcoli default), so it fires on the D-period replication clock and the dry-mass threshold is deliberately ignored (the `threshold=nan fg` in every DIVISION log line; see v2ecoli/steps/division.py:181-190). Mean division time — and hence the mu this study reads out — is therefore set by replication timing, not carbon supply, and cells divide on schedule at ~695 fg regardless of how tightly glucose is capped.

    Consequence for the instrument: an acetate-vs-growth-rate card cannot be driven by a carbon knob in this model. All six sweep points land in a ~0.03 /h band, entirely above the reference onset, so the `overflow-absent-across-titration` verdict is graded on a degenerate x-range. Adding seeds or generations will not help — the axis is structurally pinned, not noisy.
  • Under a binding glucose cap the model creates carbon. At cap=1 it takes up 6.0 mmolC/gDCW/h as glucose and secretes 11.83 mmolC/gDCW/h of carbon — 11.62 of it as CO2 — while still growing at 0.954 /h and therefore also building biomass carbon. This trips the assumption-free `violation_C_created` flag, which compares secreted C-exchange against C uptake and needs no biomass C-content assumption.

    The effect is inverted with respect to carbon supply: CO2 output RISES as glucose is restricted (11.62 / 5.00 / 1.82 mmol/gDCW/h at caps 1 / 2 / 6), and the carbon closure fraction degrades monotonically as the cap tightens (1.11 -> 7.93 across caps 6 -> 1). At the slack cap the books nearly close; the tighter the constraint, the more carbon appears from nowhere. Undiagnosed here. Candidate mechanisms: FBA infeasibility handling under a binding bound, non-FBA replenishment of metabolite pools, or per-gDCW flux normalisation against a stale mass.
  • Emergent biomass yield exceeds the stoichiometric ceiling at every cap, and the excess grows as carbon is restricted: Yxs = 0.92 / 1.04 / 1.29 / 1.71 / 2.59 / 5.30 gDW per g glucose at caps 6 -> 1, against a measured literature band of 0.355-0.444 (Varma 1994, Kavvas 2022, LaCroix 2015, Long 2017 — the four `kind: measured` rows of the ecoli-sources basal__biomass_yield slot) and a stoichiometric ceiling of 0.538 (Varma 1993, the slot's single `kind: theoretical_max` row). Even at the slack cap, where the FBA runs at its unconstrained optimum and the carbon books nearly close, the yield is ~1.7x the ceiling; at cap=1 it is ~10x.

    This is the same defect the basal vs_literature card already reports (biomass yield over the limit), but the titration shows it is not a fixed offset — it is a function of how hard the carbon constraint binds, which points at the same bookkeeping problem as `carbon-created-under-binding-uptake-cap` rather than at a mis-parameterised biomass composition.
