## 1. Reduced-target diagnostic: completions confirmed working, then a new symptom of the known limitation

**File:** `studies/flagella-02-transcription-regulation/run_diagnostic_reduced_target.py`
→ `charts/11_diagnostic_reduced_target_2000_7200s.svg`

**Why:** real completions need ≥133 min minimum (2026-08-07's feasibility
calculation), but the multi-gen window we can currently trust is only ~3
generations (~2hr) before the division/mass-homeostasis bug corrupts
results. Maya's proposal: temporarily lower the completion *target* (not
the real, cited stoichiometry) so real completions happen fast enough to
observe within that trustworthy window, and test whether the FliT
checkpoint + assembly mechanisms alone produce self-limiting flagella
count dynamics.

**Mechanism:** `target_length` dropped from 20,000 to 2,000 via
`config_overrides={"ecoli-flagella-filament-elongation.target_length": 2000}`
at `build_composite()` call time — a declarative, one-run-only override.
`complexation_reactions_modified.tsv`'s real stoichiometry (FliC
~20,000/filament, PMC7696725) is **not** touched by this script.

**Result, in two parts:**

**Part 1 (positive, new):** within the clean window (generations 1-2,
first ~46 min), the mechanism genuinely works — 5 real completions,
total flagella count climbing 4→9 (`completed_ever` 0→5). First direct
evidence the FliT checkpoint + assembly pipeline produce actual
completions, not an asymptotic stall, once completion speed itself isn't
the limiting factor.

**Part 2 (new symptom of the known limitation):** right as the
division-mass-homeostasis corruption set in (~generation 3, divisions at
582.1/593.0fg, below the normal ~700fg), completions **stopped
entirely** — `total_flag` frozen at exactly 9 for the remaining ~70 min
of the run, even as `filament_length` kept growing well *past* its own
2,000-subunit completion target (up to 8,387 — over 4x over) without ever
being marked complete.

| time | total_flag | completed_ever | max_len (target=2000) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 600s | 5 | 1 | 89 |
| 2400s | 8 | 4 | 203 |
| 3000s (gen 2) | 9 | 5 | 4,156 |
| 5400s (gen 3, post-corruption) | 9 | 5 | 7,200 |
| 7200s | 9 | 5 | 8,387 |

**So the original question (does flagella count self-limit over
generations) is still not answered** — the run ran out of clean window
before that could be observed either way. But this is real, useful new
evidence about the underlying corruption: it doesn't just depress
division mass, it appears to silently break completion-triggering logic
too, once it sets in. Not flagella-specific — read as further evidence
the corruption is a general per-cell state problem, not narrowly a mass
issue. Added to `investigation.yaml`'s `division-mass-homeostasis-gap`
open decision and a new finding
(`flagella-02-reduced-target-confirms-completion-then-freeze`) in
`studies/flagella-02-transcription-regulation/study.yaml`.

## 2. Follow-up: target length wasn't the real bottleneck — nucleation rate was, then motor-complex supply

**Files:** same `run_diagnostic_reduced_target.py`, extended with a
`--nucleation-rate` override; `check_nucleation_material.py`
(scratchpad, single-cell instrumentation, not a permanent study script).

**target=500 alone reproduced target=2000's trajectory byte-identically**
through the first division (same `completed_ever` 1,2,3,4; same
`total_flag` 5,6,7,8; same `max_len` 89,129,167,203) — revealing
nucleation rate (~1 event/600s), not target length, was actually pacing
completions in that regime. Each new filament finishes well within one
nucleation interval regardless of target, so the next nucleation event —
not filament growth — was the real bottleneck.

**Bumped `nucleation_rate` 10x too** (0.0167, same `config_overrides`
pattern — itself already a derived estimate, not a literature constant,
so this explores a faster point on an uncertain rate rather than
overriding a measured value). Result: 6 completions in the first 600s
(vs. 4 in 2400s before) — confirms rate was the bottleneck. But then
**flagella count plateaued at exactly 10 for a sustained 30 minutes**,
well within the clean pre-corruption window.

**Did not assume this plateau was the FliT/FlhDC checkpoint working** —
directly instrumented `FLAGELLAR-MOTOR-COMPLEX` and its raw materials
(FlgE/FlgK/FlgL) during the exact plateau window. Result:
`FLAGELLAR-MOTOR-COMPLEX` hits exactly 0 at t=300s and stays 0 for the
rest of the window — precisely when nucleation stops firing. FlgE/FlgK/
FlgL all stay abundant throughout (thousands of copies, never close to
limiting).

| t | motor_complex | FlgE | FlgK | FlgL | n_nascent |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 60s | 4 | 3264 | 1659 | 3342 | 1 |
| 300s | 0 | 2861 | 1609 | 3463 | 1 |
| 360s | 0 | 2880 | 1608 | 3501 | **0** |
| 2400s | 0 | 4217 | 1587 | 3495 | 0 |

**So the plateau is a second, independent supply bottleneck** —
nucleation consumes 1 motor complex per event
(`flagella_filament_nucleation.py`), the standing pool exhausts after ~5
quick nucleations, and new motor complexes aren't being assembled fast
enough (itself built from FlgH/MotA/MotB/FliF/the switch complex
CPLX0-7450/the export apparatus CPLX0-7451 — a whole separate synthesis
chain, not examined). **Not the intended regulatory mechanism.** Across
every diagnostic this session (target=2000, target=500,
target=500+10x-nucleation), the FliT/FlhDC checkpoint has never actually
been observed to be the limiting factor on flagella count — something
else (fliC supply, motor-complex supply, or the division-mass-
homeostasis corruption) has capped every run first.

**Stopping here for now**, per Maya's explicit call. Logged as a new
finding (`flagella-02-second-supply-bottleneck-motor-complex-not-
checkpoint`) and open decision (`motor-complex-supply-chain-unexamined`)
in `studies/flagella-02-transcription-regulation/study.yaml`, for
whoever picks this up next — same methodology as the fliC investigation
(real-time rate measurement, back-calculated demand, transcriptome-
budget-scoped fix) would apply to whichever motor-complex input turns
out to be most limiting.
