## 2. FliC stoichiometry feasibility check (no code change — a calculation)

**Context:** Maya asked whether FliC's ~20,000-subunit-per-flagellum
requirement should be reduced to make completions feasible within a
reasonable simulated window.

**Method:** solved Renault et al. 2017's own growth law,
`L(t) = sqrt(b²+2at) - b` (a=26,450, b=575), for the time to reach 20,000
subunits assuming **unlimited** FliC (no synthesis bottleneck at all).

**Result: 7,996.2s / 133.3 min (~2.22 hr) — the theoretical minimum, best
case.** A single ~42 min generation is shorter than this floor, so zero
completions in any single-generation test is expected, not evidence the
stoichiometry itself is unrealistic.

**Recommendation (accepted):** do not reduce the 20,000 coefficient — it's
real, cited structural biology (PMC7696725), not a parameter choice.
Either extend test windows or continue closing the fliC synthesis gap
instead.

## 3. Division-disabled diagnostic: is completion an asymptotic stall, or just a timing problem?

**Files:** `studies/flagella-02-transcription-regulation/run_diagnostic_no_division.py`
→ `charts/09_diagnostic_no_division_18000s.svg`

**Why:** every single-cell test this session hits division at t≈2,524-2,528s
(~42 min, mass-threshold-adjacent D-period timer) and crashes
(`KeyError: '0'`, agent replaced by daughters). This conflates two
questions: (1) can the fliC-corrected calibration (~6/s, still ~7.6x short
of ~46/s peak demand) ever complete a filament given enough time, or does
growth asymptotically stall as more concurrent filaments compete for a
still-limited pool; (2) does division itself prevent completion,
independent of (1)? This diagnostic isolates (1).

**Mechanism:** monkeypatches `Division.next_update` to a no-op (`{}`) for
the duration of one run only, fully reverted in a `finally` block.
Verified via direct code research: `Division.update` just calls
`self.next_update(1.0, state)`, so patching this one method suffices; no
`config_overrides` path can reach `d_period` (hardcoded `True` in
`Division.initialize`, not sourced from any override-able config).

**Result: YES, completion is achievable.** 9 flagella completed over 5
simulated hours (18,000s), first ones appearing between the 180 and
210-minute checkpoints — consistent with the 133-min analytical floor
above. Once completions start, free FliC rebounds sharply (11→120,325) as
elongation demand drops.

| time | n_nascent | max_len | free FliC | completed_ever | dry_mass |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 30 min | 4 | 9,198 | 4,383 | 0 | 595.9fg |
| 120 min | 8 | 14,381 | 1,292 | 0 | 1,887.6fg |
| 210 min | 12 | 19,842 | 32,651 | **3** | 5,760.8fg |
| 300 min | 15 | 19,883 | 120,325 | **9** | 18,130.6fg |

**Important caveat:** division being disabled let dry_mass grow unbounded
(ending ~18,131fg, ~26x a normal ~700fg division mass). Since
ribosome/RNAP counts scale with cell size in this model, later
completions (5-9, from ~240 min on) are likely inflated by this
artificially oversized cell having far more synthesis capacity than any
real dividing lineage would ever have at once. The **first** completions
(~180-210 min, cell only ~8x oversized) are the cleaner signal.

**Implication:** under current calibration, no single real (dividing)
cell can complete a flagellum within its own ~42 min lifetime.
Completion, if it happens in the real model, can only come from
inherited, in-progress construction (`nascent_flagellum`'s
`filament_length`) accumulating across **multiple** real generations, via
`divide_nascent_flagellum`'s whole-unit binomial inheritance (confirmed
2026-08-06 to survive division intact, continuing from its current
length, not restarted).

## 4. Multi-generation investigation — discovered a general WCM limitation

This is the largest finding of the session. Testing whether inherited
progress actually completes and converges to a realistic count
(Sim et al. 2017: 2.4-7.8 flagella/cell) over several real generations
required tracking a lineage across many divisions. Three attempts:

### 4a. Manual splicing (first attempt) — a real, separate bug, since fixed by switching approach

**File:** `studies/flagella-02-transcription-regulation/run_lineage_multigen.py` (superseded version)

Copied `run_studies_multigen.py`'s existing generation-chaining pattern:
call `divide_cell()` directly, rebuild a fresh `baseline()` composite,
hand-splice `bulk`/`unique`/`environment`/`boundary` onto it. **Found a
real, previously-undetected bug**: dry_mass at division drifted sharply
downward generation over generation (706.7 → 632.6 → 553.5 → ... → 262.0
fg by generation 7), eventually flooding the metabolism FBA solver with
`GLP_NOFEAS`/numerical-instability warnings and driving complete-flagella
count to 0. `run_studies_multigen.py` itself was never stress-tested past
`--generations 2` (its only existing chart, `05_multigen_flgm_flia.svg`),
so this compounding drift was never caught before.

**Fix attempted:** switch to the real `Division` step's own
daughter-construction machinery (proven correct, no manual splicing),
pruning to a single followed agent after each division — same linear
compute cost, avoids the hand-rolled state transfer entirely.

### 4b. Real Division machinery + naive pruning — hit a framework bug

First attempt at the fix used `del comp.state["agents"][agent_id]`
directly. Crashed on the very next `comp.run()`:
`AttributeError: 'NoneType' object has no attribute 'get'` in
process_bigraph's `run_steps`. **Root cause:** `process_bigraph`'s
`Composite` maintains internal step/process-path bookkeeping
(`self.process_paths`/`self.step_paths`) that raw dict deletion leaves
stale. **Fix:** use `comp.apply({"agents": {"_remove": [agent_id]}})` —
the same `"_remove"` structural-update primitive `Division.next_update`
itself uses (`division.py:439-447`), which internally calls
`self.find_instance_paths(self.state)` to refresh the registry.

### 4c. Selection-bias check — ruled out

With the removal API fixed, the SAME monotonic drift reappeared
(706.7→646.5→559.3→525.4 fg). Since "always keep the lexicographically
first agent ID" could in principle correlate with a smaller share if
`Division`'s daughter-ID assignment has any positional convention,
switched to a genuine random coin-flip (its own RNG stream, decorrelated
from ID string order) for which daughter to keep. **Drift persisted
identically** (706.7→598.9→531.0→476.7→472.6 fg across 5 generations) —
~3% probability under pure chance, and combined with the previous run's
4-decrease streak, this rules out selection-rule bias as the cause.

### 4d. Root cause investigation

Checked whether this was specific to single-lineage pruning by re-running
the (already-validated) expanding-population approach
(`run_flit_checkpoint_multigen.py`) for longer (4hr target instead of
2hr). **The identical pattern appeared** once it reached ~6 agents /
generation 4 (divisions at 513.7, 565.0, 557.2, 521.5 fg + the same
`GLP_NOFEAS` floods) — ruling out single-lineage-specific causes entirely.

**Investigated and ruled out:** shared/depleting environment state.
Direct code inspection confirmed `ecoli_baseline` wires no
`environment_mirror`/pooled-environment step (those only exist in
`ecoli_colony.py`/`ecoli_time_varying_env.py`, unused here); each agent's
`environment`/`boundary` is a private, non-shared copy, explicitly carried
at division (`division.py:148,306,388-390`); `media_update.py` is a no-op
unless `media_id` itself changes; nothing decrements concentrations based
on actual FBA uptake. So environment is a static, effectively-infinite
value regardless of agent count or elapsed time — not the cause.

**Most likely real cause (code-verified, not yet confirmed as fixable
in-session):** a compounding, per-cell feedback loop:

1. `division.py`'s default D-period timer (`self.d_period=True`) fires a
   *fixed* duration after chromosome replication completes — the
   dry-mass threshold is explicitly ignored on this path
   (`division.py:274-290`). There is no mechanism forcing an underweight
   cell to wait for adequate mass before dividing.
2. An underweight daughter (ordinary binomial variance in `divide_bulk`
   guarantees these occur routinely) divides at a proportionally smaller
   mass — it had the same fixed time to grow, no extra catch-up time.
3. That daughter's metabolism FBA problem (calibrated around a ~700fg
   reference cell) becomes more likely to go infeasible.
4. `allocator.py` documents (by design) that an infeasible FBA tick can
   drive a bulk pool negative, and explicitly does **not** heal it
   afterward ("the negative pool is the upstream's to heal") — the
   corruption persists and is inherited unmodified by the next
   generation via ordinary `divide_bulk`.
5. That corrupted starting point makes the next generation grow more
   slowly, dividing even smaller — repeat, compounding.

This explains why it only appears after 3-4 generations (needs time to
accumulate) and why it's identical whether tracking one lineage or the
whole population (it's a per-agent property, not population-size-
dependent).

**Decision (Maya, 2026-08-07): document as a known limitation for now,
do not attempt a fix in-session.** This is a real, separate engineering
effort (either adding mass-gated correction to the division timer, or
making the allocator heal negative pools from infeasible solves), not a
quick patch.

**Practical implication for all future work in this repo, not just
flagella:** any multi-generation result should be treated as trustworthy
only within roughly the first 3 generations (~2hr from a typical
starting condition). Every result generated earlier in this session's
work (the 2hr/4-agent population run, all single-cell tests, the
division-disabled diagnostic) stayed within or used a controlled
workaround for this window and remains valid. Longer, untested runs
should be treated with suspicion until this is actually fixed.

## Where this leaves the original question

The core question this investigation has been chasing — does inherited,
in-progress flagellar construction complete and converge to a realistic
count (2.4-7.8/cell) over several real generations — is **still open**.
What's now known:
- Completion is achievable given enough uninterrupted time (~180+ min per
  filament, confirmed directly).
- A single generation (~42 min) can never be enough on its own — multi-
  generation inheritance is structurally required.
- Testing that multi-generation hypothesis directly is currently blocked
  by the division/mass-homeostasis limitation above, unrelated to
  flagella, once pushed past ~3-4 generations.

Logged as open decisions in both `investigation.yaml`
(`division-mass-homeostasis-gap`, the general limitation) and
`studies/flagella-02-transcription-regulation/study.yaml`
(`multigen-flagella-completion-still-untested-past-3-generations`, the
flagella-specific consequence).
