Question & approach

Question. Across a glucose-uptake-rate titration, does v2ecoli reproduce the aerobic acetate-overflow response — the acetate-carbon yield rising ~linearly with growth rate above a critical threshold (Vemuri 2006 primary, Basan 2015 context) — and how do the FBA baseline, the Millard kinetic ODE, and the v2ecoli+Millard FBA-bridge arms compare?

Biology — what this study is about

Derived from findings (shown until you write an override):

The v2ecoli baseline FBA exhibits NO aerobic acetate overflow anywhere in a glucose-uptake titration: acetate is identically 0.0 mmol/gDCW/h at all six caps, so the acetate-carbon yield stays flat at 0 while the Vemuri 2006 reference rises to ~0.25 above an onset of 0.408 /h. This confirms the pre-registered prediction (and the ketchup-baseline-comparison prior that v2ecoli secretes 0.0 acetate per 100 glucose where the KETCHUP cores secrete ~+71): overflow is a constraint phenomenon, and unconstrained growth-max FBA respires fully rather than spilling carbon.

THIS FINDING REFUTES THIS STUDY'S OWN SECOND DESIGN ASSUMPTION — that a glucose-uptake titration moves the model along a growth-rate axis comparable to the references'. It is FALSE for v2ecoli. This is a refutation of our premise, not a disagreement with a reference. Across a 5.6x uptake range the emergent growth rate moves only 0.954 -> 0.925 /h, non-monotonically. The cause is by design, not a bug: division runs with d_period=True (the vEcoli default), so it fires on the D-period replication clock and the dry-mass threshold is deliberately ignored (the `threshold=nan fg` in every DIVISION log line; see v2ecoli/steps/division.py:181-190). Mean division time — and hence the mu this study reads out — is therefore set by replication timing, not carbon supply, and cells divide on schedule at ~695 fg regardless of how tightly glucose is capped. Consequence for the instrument: an acetate-vs-growth-rate card cannot be driven by a carbon knob in this model. All six sweep points land in a ~0.03 /h band, entirely above the reference onset, so the `overflow-absent-across-titration` verdict is graded on a degenerate x-range. Adding seeds or generations will not help — the axis is structurally pinned, not noisy.

Under a binding glucose cap the model creates carbon. At cap=1 it takes up 6.0 mmolC/gDCW/h as glucose and secretes 11.83 mmolC/gDCW/h of carbon — 11.62 of it as CO2 — while still growing at 0.954 /h and therefore also building biomass carbon. This trips the assumption-free `violation_C_created` flag, which compares secreted C-exchange against C uptake and needs no biomass C-content assumption. The effect is inverted with respect to carbon supply: CO2 output RISES as glucose is restricted (11.62 / 5.00 / 1.82 mmol/gDCW/h at caps 1 / 2 / 6), and the carbon closure fraction degrades monotonically as the cap tightens (1.11 -> 7.93 across caps 6 -> 1). At the slack cap the books nearly close; the tighter the constraint, the more carbon appears from nowhere. Undiagnosed here. Candidate mechanisms: FBA infeasibility handling under a binding bound, non-FBA replenishment of metabolite pools, or per-gDCW flux normalisation against a stale mass.

Emergent biomass yield exceeds the stoichiometric ceiling at every cap, and the excess grows as carbon is restricted: Yxs = 0.92 / 1.04 / 1.29 / 1.71 / 2.59 / 5.30 gDW per g glucose at caps 6 -> 1, against a measured literature band of 0.355-0.444 (Varma 1994, Kavvas 2022, LaCroix 2015, Long 2017 — the four `kind: measured` rows of the ecoli-sources basal__biomass_yield slot) and a stoichiometric ceiling of 0.538 (Varma 1993, the slot's single `kind: theoretical_max` row). Even at the slack cap, where the FBA runs at its unconstrained optimum and the carbon books nearly close, the yield is ~1.7x the ceiling; at cap=1 it is ~10x. This is the same defect the basal vs_literature card already reports (biomass yield over the limit), but the titration shows it is not a fixed offset — it is a function of how hard the carbon constraint binds, which points at the same bookkeeping problem as `carbon-created-under-binding-uptake-cap` rather than at a mis-parameterised biomass composition.

Plan & provenance

Key assumptions

  • The acetate exchange flux (ACET[p], listeners.fba_results.external_exchange_fluxes) is the model observable for overflow; growth rate is read from the mass listener.
  • A glucose-uptake-rate titration moves the model along a growth-rate axis comparable to the references', on the existing glucose ParCa cache — no new data bundle needed for this condition. (Mirrors Basan's transporter-titration causal direction: set uptake -> growth emerges.)
  • The OD600->gDCW factor used to convert Basan's reported Jac (mM/OD600/h) to the model's acetate exchange flux (mmol/gDCW/h) is an EXTERNAL assumption (not in Basan 2015) — either import a sourced NCM3722 factor or grade in per-OD units by converting the model side. See inputs/notes/basan2015.md.
