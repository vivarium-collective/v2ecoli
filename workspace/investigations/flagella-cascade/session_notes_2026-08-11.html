01Regulatory biology

Removing the FliT:FlhDC checkpoint

Real, literature-grounded biology in Salmonella — but no significant phenotype in this WCM's exact E. coli strain.

FliT is a chaperone that, in Salmonella, sequesters FlhD4C2 (the flagellar master regulator complex) as a negative-feedback checkpoint once enough flagellar substrate has accumulated.

Yamamoto & Kutsukake (2006), J Bacteriol 188:5124 — FliT-mediated feedback checkpoint on FlhD4C2, established in Salmonella.

Why it was removed

Albanna et al. (2018), Sci Rep 8:16705 directly tested a ΔfliT mutant in E. coli K-12 MG1655 — the exact reference strain this WCM is built on — and found no significant phenotypic effect, in contrast to the clear effect documented in Salmonella. The mechanism's real-world significance in K-12 specifically is doubtful.

What was removed

Code

flagella_flit_flhdc_checkpoint.py — the fast-equilibrium FliT–FlhD4C2 binding Step

flagella_flhdc_degradation.py — basal ClpXP-mediated FlhD4C2 turnover (removed together, since it isn't actually FliT-specific — it was FlhD4C2's only decay pathway)

Reaction network

FLIT-FLID-CPLX_RXN removed from equilibrium_reactions.tsv / rates

FLIT-DIMER_RXN kept — real, independent FliT homodimer biology (Yamamoto & Kutsukake 2006), now unconsumed, kept as an NFsim building block

Everything removed is preserved at archive/flit-flhdc-regulation-2026-08/ per the standing preserve-old-code rule.

The accepted tradeoff

FlhD4C2 now has no degradation pathway at all. Ordinary protein_degradation only handles monomers, never assembled complexes. This reopens the flagella-count-unbounded-runaway risk that motivated the original checkpoint work — an explicit, informed decision, not an oversight, pending a planned NFsim rule-based replacement (flagella-04-complexation-nfsim).

Accepted tradeoff — no quick-fix cap applied
02Architecture

Execution order of the flagella_regulation feature

Seven Steps, fixed order, every tick — inserted as a block immediately before transcript initiation, and (critically) before the ordinary complexation process.

motor-switch-
assembly→ export-apparatus-
assembly→ motor-complex-
assembly→ filament-

elongation→ flgm-
secretion→ transcription-
regulation

Second box (export-apparatus-assembly) is new this session — see slide 06.

Where this sits in the tick

Registered via insert_before: 'ecoli-transcript-initiation' in ecoli_baseline.py. Each Step is inserted as its own execution layer immediately before the layer containing ecoli-transcript-initiation — which is the same layer as ecoli-complexation (the ordinary Gillespie SSA process for the rest of the cell's complex formation).

Layer 4b (base): [ecoli-complexation, ecoli-chromosome-replication, ecoli-polypeptide-initiation, ecoli-transcript-initiation] With flagella_regulation enabled, six/seven new layers are inserted immediately BEFORE that layer — so every flagella Step runs before ecoli-complexation, every single tick.

This ordering fact is the key to two separate findings later in these notes: why CPLX0-7450/CPLX0-7451 are structurally invisible to external sampling (slide 09), and why leaving CPLX0-7451_RXN in ordinary SSA created a cross-mechanism race once it depended on a Step-only intermediate (slide 06).

03Filament biology

FliC target length: 20,000 → 10,000 → 5,000

Two deliberate reductions, both real values inside the literature range, both driven by directly observed FliC pool depletion.

Real filament length: ~20,000–40,000 FliC (flagellin) subunits, 5–20 μm, at ~2,000–2,130 subunits/μm (PMC7696725).

The growth law

Renault et al. (2017), eLife 6:e23136, "Bacterial flagella grow through an injection–diffusion mechanism" — filament growth is length-dependent, not constant, as diffusion through the growing channel becomes rate-limiting:

dL/dt = a / (b + L) a ≈ 26,450 subunit²/s, b ≈ 575 subunits T(L) ≈ (b·L + L²/2) / a (completion time, integrating from 0)
target_lengthcompletion time T(L)why it moved
20,000 (original)~133 minreal value, but exceeds a whole generation (~42 min)
10,000~35 minstill real (short end of range); single-gen completion becomes achievable
5,000 (current)~9.7 minfree FliC nearly fully drained at 10,000 (51,967 → 14 in one generation) — halved again for real headroom
Observed at target=10,000 Free FliC: 51,967 → 14 over 2400s, with the longest filament only 85% complete (8,540/10,000). Pool on the verge of total exhaustion within a single generation.
At target=5,000 (v9–v11) Same window: free FliC ends around 900–2,300 depending on run — real, survivable headroom, with 3–4 flagella completing per generation on a clean ~600s cadence.
Fixed — both cuts literature-grounded, not arbitrary
04Structural stoichiometry

Export apparatus (CPLX0-7451) — a two-round fix

Checked against two of Maya's own reference spreadsheets, then against real structural literature component-by-component — because "matches the spreadsheet" turned out not to be sufficient.

CPLX0-7451_RXN was a vendored, previously-untouched EcoCyc reaction — most components sitting at ParCa's null-stoichiometry default (-1), never checked this investigation.

Round 1 — FliP:FliQ:FliR

Before FliP : FliQ : FliR = 1 : 1 : 1 — the vendored null default, and (surprisingly) also both of Maya's spreadsheets' unsourced default.
After FliP : FliQ : FliR = 5 : 4 : 1 — real cryo-EM-determined stoichiometry of the Salmonella FliPQR core export-gate complex.
Kuhlen et al. (2018), Nat Struct Mol Biol; building on Fukumura et al. (2017), PLOS Biol 15:e2002281 (PMC5542437), 4.2 Å cryo-EM.

Round 2 — FlhA (follow-up literature check)

Before FlhA = −1 — again, both spreadsheets' unsourced default matched the vendored null default.
After FlhA = −9 — a well-established homo-nonameric ring in the export gate.
PLOS One 10.1371/journal.pone.0252800 (FlhA cytoplasmic domain nonamer), corroborated by an independent comprehensive stoichiometry statement: "nine FlhA subunits, one FlhB subunit, five FliP subunits, four FliQ subunits, and one FliR subunit" — which simultaneously re-confirmed FlhB/FliP/FliQ/FliR.

Independently confirmed correct as-is: FliH=−12, FliI=−6, FliJ=−1 — a literature source names this exact "FliH12-FliI6-FliJ1" stoichiometry, a three-way match between spreadsheets, existing code, and literature.

Left open, deliberately

FliO = −1, unchanged. Literature describes FliO as a transient assembly scaffold — it helps the FliP ring assemble correctly, then is not retained in the mature complex (a ΔfliO mutant's motility is rescued to wild-type by FliP overexpression alone). This raises a real structural-modeling question — does FliO belong as a consumed reactant in the reaction forming the final complex at all? — that a copy-number fix can't resolve. Logged as an open decision, not answered this session, per explicit instruction.

Open question — export-apparatus-flio-scaffold-role-unresolved
Final CPLX0-7451_RXN (current, v11): CPLX0-7451 + CPLX0-7450 → G370(FlhA)×9 + G7028(FlhB)×1 + EG11224(FliO)×1(open Q) + EG11975(FliP)×5 + EG11976(FliQ)×4 + EG11977(FliR)×1 + EG11656(FliH)×12 + G377(FliI)×6 + G378(FliJ)×1

Standing lesson: a value matching Maya's own working spreadsheet is not, on its own, verification — both her spreadsheets shared the same unsourced default that literature contradicted for FliP/Q/R and FlhA. Independent literature checks are required even when a spreadsheet value already agrees.

05Assembly hierarchy

C-ring → export apparatus: fixing a missing dependency

The two complexes were modeled as independent, parallel branches — real biology says one depends on the other.

Minamino & Namba (2008), Nature; Chevance & Hughes (2008), Nat Rev Microbiol — real assembly order: MS-ring (FliF) forms first → C-ring (FliG/FliM/FliN) assembles onto its cytoplasmic face → the export apparatus inserts into the pre-formed C-ring's central pore.
Before CPLX0-7450 (C-ring) and CPLX0-7451 (export apparatus) formed independently from raw monomers, only merging downstream when FLAGELLAR-MOTOR-COMPLEX_RXN consumed both.
After CPLX0-7451_RXN now consumes CPLX0-7450 (−1) directly — the export apparatus can't form until the C-ring already exists. The redundant direct CPLX0-7450 consumption was removed from the final motor-complex reaction to avoid double-counting.

First result (v9) — real progress, new problem

3 flagella completed in one generation (vs. only 1 pre-fix) on a clean ~600s cadence. But the motor-complex pool drained monotonically — 6→5→4→4→3 — instead of holding steady. See slide 06 for why, and its fix.

Fixed — real dependency wired in
06Architecture bug, found and fixed same day

The SSA-vs-deterministic-Step race

Correcting the biology exposed a mismatch between two different execution mechanisms running the same reaction network.

CPLX0-7451_RXN was never excluded from ordinary Gillespie SSA (ecoli-complexation) — its largest coefficient (FlhA×9) was never numerically dangerous, unlike the three reactions already pulled out for combinatorial propensity blow-up (FliN×111, FliC×5,000–20,000).

The slide-05 fix made CPLX0-7451_RXN depend on CPLX0-7450 — a molecule that only ever exists transiently inside one deterministic Step's single tick (see slide 02's execution-order diagram: all flagella Steps run before ecoli-complexation, same tick). SSA's own timing had no reliable way to catch it before the next tick started it over.

CPLX0-7451 mechanismresult observed (v9)
Before slide-05 fixSSA, independent of CPLX0-7450steady accumulation (21→32 over run)
After slide-05 fix, before this fixSSA, gated on Step-only CPLX0-7450replenishment stalls — motor-complex pool 6→3

The fix

Moved CPLX0-7451_RXN out of ecoli-complexation entirely, into a new deterministic Step — flagella_export_apparatus_assembly.py — matching the same pattern already used for the other three flagella reactions. Added to RUNTIME_EXCLUDED_REACTIONS.

v9 (SSA race, unfixed) Motor-complex pool: 6 → 5 → 4 → 4 → 3 over 2400s. Never replenished.
v10 (Step conversion) Motor-complex pool: oscillates 5 – 6 the whole run. 4 flagella completed — the best of any version this session.

FlhA remained the real, biologically-grounded bottleneck throughout — this fix removed the artifact race condition on top of it, not the underlying scarcity (see slide 10 for what happened to that bottleneck next).

Fixed — architectural consistency, not a numerical necessity
07Assembly hierarchy, continued

MS-ring ordering fix — the same gap, one stage upstream

The same citation used for slide 05 also implies FliF was in the wrong place.

Minamino & Namba (2008), Nature; Chevance & Hughes (2008), Nat Rev Microbiol — the MS-ring (FliF) forms first, before the C-ring ever assembles onto it.
Before FliF (×34) consumed directly in FLAGELLAR-MOTOR-COMPLEX_RXN, the very last assembly stage — lumped in alongside unrelated stator/rod/ring parts (FlgH, FlgI, MotA, MotB, FlgB/C/F/G).
After FliF folded into CPLX0-7450_RXN alongside FliG/FliM/FliN. CPLX0-7450 now represents the merged MS-ring + C-ring structure — a reasonable single-stage merge, since structural papers often describe these as forming one continuous ring rather than two independently-isolable intermediates.
CPLX0-7450_RXN (current, v11): CPLX0-7450 → FliF(MS-ring)×34 + FliG×34 + FliM×34 + FliN×111

Result

Bundled into the same rebuild (v11) as the nucleation fix (slide 08). Motor-complex pool held 6–7 for the first 600s using the initial protein stockpile (now correctly including FliF), then settled into the same healthy 5–6 oscillation as v10 — confirming the reordering introduced no new bottleneck.

Fixed — verified against real single-gen rerun
08Code correctness

Nucleation's "free" first event

A shared scheduling default meant the one Step designed to be rare wasn't rare on its very first firing.

flagella_filament_nucleation.py is deliberately rate-limited — it should fire roughly once every ~600s, grounded in real biology (existing structures preferentially absorb material over nucleating new ones).

Chang, Sung & Hong (2025), Biochem Biophys Reports 42:102051 — "Intrinsic clustering of flagellar basal body proteins in E. coli."

The bug

update_condition: next_update_time <= global_time next_update_time defaults to 0.0 == global_time at t=0 ↓ 0.0 <= 0.0 is TRUE → fires immediately, first tick, skipping the wait

Every other flagella Step wants this default (fire immediately, every tick, no waiting) — but nucleation specifically does not. The result: every single-generation run got one "free" nucleation event at simulation start that bypassed the intended ~600s wait, while every subsequent event correctly waited the full interval.

The fix

In update(), the very first call (detected via the existing lazy bulk-index-resolution check) now only schedules next_update_time one full interval ahead and returns — no flagellum created. Every call after that behaves exactly as before.

Before (v10) First nascent flagellum appears at t≈120s, already 2,016 subunits grown by the first sample.
After (v11) n_nascent stays 0 until t=600s exactly — matching the intended wait, verified directly against the real trajectory.
Fixed — verified via real data, not code review alone
09Observability & the full panel

Why CPLX0-7450 / CPLX0-7451 always read 0

Not a bug — a structural consequence of running the whole chain deterministically, in one fixed order, every tick.

Every flagella Step now runs in the same tick, in the same fixed order (slide 02): each one's output is fully consumed by the next before external state is ever sampled. Nothing in this pipeline can ever be caught mid-flight from outside a tick — the same producer-consumer trap first diagnosed for CPLX0-7450 back on 2026-08-06, now structurally guaranteed across the whole chain by design.

The workaround: plot the monomer supply instead

The raw monomers feeding these complexes are externally visible — they're built gradually by translation and only partially consumed per tick, so they show real, honest line-plot dynamics. Two new panels were added to the single-generation chart for exactly this reason.

The n_nascent panel sits flat at 1 for almost the entire run — also not an error. At the current 5,000-subunit target, completion time (~581–600s) and the nucleation interval (~599s) are coincidentally almost equal, so a new flagellum starts the instant the previous one finishes. Raising target_length back toward 10,000+ would surface visibly concurrent construction, at the cost of the FliC headroom slide 03 was fixed for — a deliberate, undecided tradeoff, not pursued this session.

10Correction to an earlier finding

The bottleneck moved: FlhA → FliN

The new monomer-supply panels overturned something stated earlier this session — worth recording precisely.

Cache v8 (pre-hierarchy-fix) FlhA (G370-MONOMER): 1–8 copies. Genuinely scarce — CPLX0-7451_RXN fired via SSA and drew it down aggressively and often.
Cache v11 (current) FlhA: 105–190 copies, steady all run. Ample. CPLX0-7451_RXN now only fires when gated by CPLX0-7450, which is itself gated by FliN — so FlhA is drawn on far less often, and its pool recovered.

The real bottleneck: FliN

FliN and FliM are transcribed on the same operon (fliLMNOPQR, TU0-1441) — produced in lockstep, same mRNA bursts. The only reason their standing pools diverge is stoichiometric: FliN costs 111 per C-ring event vs. FliM's 34, so each firing of motor_switch_assembly drains FliN's pool by a proportionally much bigger bite.

Recommendation: leave FliN alone. The co-transcription fact rules out a synthesis-rate-fitting error; the stoichiometry is already literature-cited (111±13, PMC10128058); and the motor-complex pool it ultimately feeds has stayed healthy (5–7) in every run this session — meaning this bottleneck doesn't actually constrain the output metric that matters (flagella completions). No evidence it needs fixing.

11Diagnostic technique

Seeing the invisible: the isolation diagnostic

The monkeypatch technique that first proved the same-tick producer-consumer chain, back when CPLX0-7450 alone was the mystery.

To directly observe an intermediate that's normally fully drained within a single tick, one consuming Step's update() is temporarily monkeypatched to a no-op for one diagnostic run — its input then has nothing removing it, and accumulates visibly.

This diagnostic is honest about what it shows: "what accumulates if nothing downstream consumes it," not the true live system. It's a research tool for understanding production rate and bottleneck identity — the monomer-supply panels (slide 09) are the non-invasive alternative for showing the live dynamics without disabling anything.

13Where we stopped

Deliberate hold  nucleation_rate vs. target_length tradeoff — holding target_length at 5,000 to preserve single-gen completions and FliC headroom; the more realistic concurrent-construction dynamic (raising target_length) was consciously deferred (slide 09).

Not yet re-verified against this session's v11 cache and fixes (every run this session stayed under the ~2520s division boundary on purpose) — re-verification explicitly deferred, not yet started.

Recommendation: not yet  NFsim migration. Revisit once the deterministic-Step approach hits something it genuinely can't represent.
