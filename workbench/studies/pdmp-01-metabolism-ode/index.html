Question & approach

Question. Can the Millard et al. 2017 kinetic ODE of E. coli central carbon and energy metabolism, wrapped in a linear-quadratic regulator (LQR) outer control layer for growth-rate regulation, replace v2ecoli's multi-objective FBA metabolism (biomass + homeostatic + kinetic-constraint, via `wholecell.utils.modular_fba`) while reproducing the Phase 0 interface statistics within tolerance across ≥3 environmental conditions?
Summary. Replaces v2ecoli's multi-objective FBA central-carbon metabolism with the Millard 2017 mechanistic kinetic ODE (glycolysis, PPP, TCA, oxidative phosphorylation), coupled to the WCM either via a consumption_matched growth driver or by pinning the ODE-predicted central fluxes into the WCM FBA LP. On M9-glucose the closed-loop cell_mass distribution matches the Phase-0 ensemble within ±σ; the ~20 fg offset was traced to open-loop water drift (fraction had climbed to 0.7045), not the kinetics or control, and the fix regulates WATER[c] closed-loop to hold the birth water fraction (target_water_mass = dry*f/(1-f)). The FBA-bridge flux-pin keeps the WCM viable (dry_mass 379.8 -> 389.8 fg over 120 s).

Alternate hypotheses (1)

The LQR consumption_matched driver reintroduces a teleonomic growth target in disguise, so the ODE only "matches" the Phase-0 ensemble because it is steered there, not because the kinetics reproduce it.
A driver that tracks a growth-rate reference can absorb model error into its control signal, masking ODE inadequacy; the match is currently demonstrated only with the driver engaged.
