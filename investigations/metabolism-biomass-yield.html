A fresh 4x8 run on a recorded commit gives Yxs 0.831 gDW/g glucose - 1.87x the top of the measured band and 1.55x the first-principles ceiling - reproducing the committed fixture's 0.826 to within 0.67%, so the violation is real and not an artefact of the fixtures' unrecorded provenance. Main claim. The v2ecoli baseline violates the first-principles ceiling on biomass yield while still conserving carbon, which localises the baseline defect to the energetic side of metabolism rather than to mass bookkeeping.

Evidence for

  • S1, fresh 4x8 run at commit 84383530, 20 post-burn-in cells, direct mass balance: Yxs = 0.8313 gDW/g glucose (CV 1.44%) against a measured band of 0.355-0.444 and a theoretical_max of 0.538 - 1.87x the top of the band and 1.55x the ceiling.
  • The committed fixture reproduces: 0.8313 fresh vs 0.8257 baked, a 0.67% difference inside the ensemble CV, so the 2026-06-17 verdict is not an artefact of the fixtures' unrecorded provenance.
  • Carbon conserves at baseline: implied biomass carbon 0.4549 gC/gDW sits inside the 0.45-0.50 range a real E. coli has, so this is not mass creation.
  • The baseline barely respires: O2:glucose 0.096, CO2:glucose 0.331, RQ 3.43 against ~1 for full oxidation, leaving 94.5% of glucose carbon in biomass and almost none burned for maintenance and ATP.

Caveats

  • The 0.826 figure comes from committed fixtures stamped reconstructed: True, commit: None, sweep_dir: None, baked: unknown. The code and sweep that produced it were never recorded, so it cannot be attributed to any particular revision. Removing that ambiguity is S1's first duty.
  • The equivalence reference mode cannot see this defect. v2ecoli is a port of v1 with byte-level equivalence demonstrated, so if both share the defect the card returns within_tol on a thermodynamically forbidden number. Correctness evidence has to come from the literature / first-principles side.

  1. Is the baseline yield violation one defect with the binding-cap carbon creation, or two?
    The overflow study's finding concludes the violation points at the same bookkeeping problem as carbon-created-under-binding-uptake-cap. That is the hypothesis S1 tests, not its premise. The signatures differ: at a slack cap carbon closure is 1.11 (books nearly close) while yield is ~2x the ceiling; under a binding cap closure degrades to 7.93 and carbon is created. Model changes

Key assumptions

  • A single 4-seed x 8-generation run with generation_lower_bound 3 (the 20 post-burn-in cells the committed fixtures record) is a like-for-like re-measurement of the blessed ensemble - same config, same cache, same burn-in window - so a divergence in Yxs is attributable to code or to the unrecorded provenance of the fixtures rather than to a different experimental design.
  • ★ No absolute plausibility band is claimed for the implied biomass carbon content, on purpose. The only in-repo range for it (~0.45-0.50 gC/gDW, scripts/render_basal_vs_ literature.py:77) is an uncited constant, its units may be a slip from biomass YIELD (gDW/g glucose), whose curated band happens to overlap the same numbers, and the repo contradicts itself elsewhere (tests/test_basal_vs_literature.py:122 uses 0.40-0.55). So this study makes no absolute claim and rests only on the relative contrast against the binding-cap condition. Grading the content absolutely needs the model's OWN composition-derived carbon content as the reference - an internal consistency check requiring no literature - which is left to a later study (see the investigation's decisions_needed).
  • ★ The carbon-closure evidence here is NOT the same statistic as the overflow titration's 1.11 / 7.93. Those are closure ratios computed against an independent estimate of biomass carbon. This study instead computes biomass carbon BY DIFFERENCE (glucose_C - CO2_C - 2*acetate_C), which makes the closure ratio 1.0 by construction and moves all the information into the implied carbon CONTENT - 0.4549 gC/gDW here. That number is the real test: if the model were creating carbon at baseline the by-difference content would come out implausible or negative, and it does not. So this is strong positive evidence for two distinct defects, but it is not a like-for-like comparison against the overflow numbers. Making it like-for-like needs the overflow runner's closure estimator, which lives only on the overflow branch and is deliberately out of scope here.

  • The baseline ensemble makes 0.826 gDW of biomass per gram of glucose consumed — 1.86× the top of the measured band (0.355–0.444) and 1.54× the first-principles ceiling of 0.538. No carbon- and energy-balanced cell can exceed that ceiling, so this is a model defect, not a calibration mismatch. The dispersion is tight (CV 1.55% across 20 cells), so it is a systematic property of the model rather than noise. Carbon is nonetheless conserved at baseline — implied biomass carbon ≈ 0.457 gC/gDW is physically plausible — which locates the defect on the energetic side: the ensemble barely respires (O₂:glucose ≈ 0.09, CO₂:glucose ≈ 0.33, RQ ≈ 3.6 against ~1 for full oxidation), so almost no glucose carbon is burned for maintenance and ATP, and nearly all of it is retained as biomass.
