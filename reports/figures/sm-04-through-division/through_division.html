Rolling the emulator through division

sm-00 halted every trajectory at division; every sm-0x conclusion was conditioned on pre-division single-cell rollouts. sm-04 follows one daughter lineage across the reset and asks whether the emulators that win within a generation still hold across the cell_mass halving.

Finding (negative, and informative): every emulator — trivial or neural — emulates coarse growth within a generation (linear teacher-forced one-step nRMSE 0.0006) but none represents the division discontinuity. At the division tick the true cell_mass drops by ~50% (a clean halving), yet each model, fed the true pre-division state, predicts continued smooth growth — the linear model's one-step error at the boundary is 3.6 nRMSE, ~6204× its within-generation error. The emulator does not follow the halving; it has no state variable that encodes 'about to divide', so autoregressive rollout sails straight through and full-lineage rollout error jumps ~8× (within-gen 0.10 → through-division 0.81). This extends the investigation's scoping result to the cell-cycle boundary: the observable-view emulator is valid only within a generation. Crossing division needs an explicit division event/reset — the motivation for the latent encode–decode follow-up.
